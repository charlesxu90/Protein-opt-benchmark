## Main-text Methods (≈360 words)

**AlphaVariant.** We propose AlphaVariant, a directed-evolution
benchmark method that couples a GPT-REINFORCE sequence generator with an
ensemble fitness surrogate and CLADE-2 style cluster-based wet-lab batch
selection. In each of five rounds, AlphaVariant proposes 96 candidate
sequences to a fitness oracle (total budget 480 queries per run). The
generator is a small transformer (≈ 0.8 M parameters) initialised from
an MSA-pretrained prior and updated by REINFORCE against a UCB
acquisition score over the surrogate ensemble. The surrogate is an
average of four base regressors (Ridge, Bayesian Ridge, Random Forest,
Gradient Boosting) trained on the cumulative oracle-queried set with
one-hot mutation features. The first round is initialised by uniformly
random sampling across the candidate library. From round 2 onward, the
generator is fine-tuned for 500 REINFORCE steps per round; the
sequences emitted during that fine-tune form a pool of ≈ 1,000–1,500
unique candidates, from which the round's 96 oracle queries are drawn
by KMeans clustering on one-hot mutation features followed by
within-cluster sampling weighted by predicted fitness. AlphaVariant is
configured identically across all four benchmark landscapes; we deliberately
avoid per-landscape hyperparameter tuning to make the comparison to
competing methods fair. We additionally implemented three optional
extensions — (i) PLM reward shaping, which augments the REINFORCE reward
with an ESM-2 wild-type-marginal log-probability term that decays
linearly across rounds; (ii) XGBoost-and-SHAP-based per-position
alphabet pruning that activates once at least 150 oracle labels have
been collected; and (iii) a weighted-allocation hybrid selector that
re-allocates the 96 per-round picks across clusters proportional to a
softmax-of-cluster-best score. We evaluated each extension as an
isolated modification at 30 random seeds against the AlphaVariant base
(Methods Fig. 3; Supplementary Fig. S2; Supplementary Table 2). None of
the three extensions significantly improved over the base on any of the
four landscapes at α = 0.10 (paired Wilcoxon); on PhoQ, PLM reward
shaping was *significantly worse* (Δ = -0.10, p = 0.003). We therefore
report AlphaVariant in the main figure with the base configuration only
and document the extensions in Supplementary Methods §S1 for future-work
hooks on landscape classes with characteristics distinct from those
covered by our benchmark set.

## Supplementary Methods §S1 — The three AlphaVariant extensions and a failed *a priori* selection rule (≈ 600 words)

**Three optional extensions** were implemented to test whether
landscape-specific machinery could improve over the AlphaVariant base.

*PLM-reward shaping.* The REINFORCE reward signal in round *r* is
augmented from the UCB acquisition score to
`reward = z(UCB) + λ(r) · z(log P_ESM)`, where `log P_ESM` is the
ESM-2 35M wild-type-marginal log-probability of a candidate at the
varying positions (Meier et al., 2021), each term z-normalised within
the batch. We tested a linear decay `λ(r) = λ_0 · max(0, (N − 1 − r) /
(N − 2))` with `λ_0 = 0.5` and `N = 5` rounds, so that PLM influence
ramps from 0.5 in round 2 to 0 in round 5. The intuition is that an
information-poor surrogate trained on round-1 random samples benefits
from a biological-plausibility regulariser early; once the surrogate has
accumulated labels, the PLM signal is faded out.

*SHAP-based alphabet pruning.* At the start of every round once at least
150 oracle labels have been collected, we fit an XGBoost regressor on the
one-hot mutation features of all collected sequences (cumulative across
rounds) and compute TreeSHAP contributions per (position, amino-acid)
feature. Amino acids whose mean SHAP contribution falls below zero at a
position are dropped from the candidate alphabet for the remainder of the
run; the candidate pool generated by the GPT in subsequent rounds is
filtered against this dynamically-pruned alphabet. The intent is to
allow the surrogate's own learned feature attributions to shrink the
candidate space.

*Weighted hybrid selection.* The 96 per-round picks are taken from the
GPT proposal pool by KMeans clustering on one-hot features followed by
slot allocation. The hybrid variant allocates slots per cluster k
proportional to `softmax(combined_max_k / T)` where `combined_max_k` is
the maximum z(UCB) + α · z(log P_ESM) within cluster k, T is a softmax
temperature, and a minimum of one slot per cluster is enforced to
preserve diversity. We tested α = 0.3 and T ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0}.

**A failed *a priori* selection rule.** We initially hypothesised that
the best extension for a given landscape could be selected from three
descriptors computed on the unlabelled library (ESM-2 WT-marginal
log-prob gap between the global-max variant and the library median;
coefficient of variation of round-1 fitness; minimum per-position
Shannon entropy among the top-128 measured variants). The rule was
inverted in 4/4 cases (Supplementary Table 1): on PhoQ, the descriptor
profile predicted PLM-reward as the best extension, but PLM-reward in
fact *significantly degraded* PhoQ performance (Δ = -0.10, paired
Wilcoxon p = 0.003); on TEV, the descriptors predicted SHAP pruning,
but PLM-reward actually had the highest mean delta. We therefore
abandoned the rule-based selection. The negative result is reported for
completeness and indicates that the mechanism by which an extension
helps a given landscape is **not** captured by the three descriptors we
proposed; in particular, PLM-reward appears to act as a
surrogate-noise regulariser rather than a wild-type-proximity prior,
explaining why it can help landscapes whose optimum is *unlike* the
wild type (TEV) and hurt landscapes whose optimum is *like* the
wild type (PhoQ).

**Statistical protocol.** All extensions were evaluated at n = 30
random seeds drawn from `rand_seeds.txt`. Comparisons against the
AlphaVariant base used paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the
seed-matched max-fitness deltas; significance threshold α = 0.10
(Supplementary Table 2).

## Discussion limitation (≈ 90 words)

> A natural next step is to evaluate AlphaVariant on landscapes outside
> our four-benchmark set — most usefully an antibody library or a
> longer combinatorial library (≥ 10 variable positions) — where the
> base AlphaVariant configuration may benefit from one of the
> extensions we tested. Our negative result on the rule-based selector
> implies that mechanistic understanding of *why* a given extension
> helps a given landscape is still incomplete; we expect this to be a
> productive direction once a larger benchmark of dataset/extension
> outcomes is available.

> baselines on three of four combinatorial landscapes.** Bars show
